Bottom line up front. TRIZ is a real, 1946-origin patent-mining heuristic with one genuinely useful core move — explicit contradiction formulation + separation/IFR reasoning — and a large surrounding apparatus (matrix, Su-Field, ARIZ, 76 standards, evolution laws) whose evidence base in software, ops, and AI is thin and dominated by self-reported consultancy cases. A3. The evidence base — and its weaknesses

This is where honesty matters most. The evidence landscape is thin, mostly self-reported, and methodologically weak.

Controlled / comparative studies (the better end):

Ge et al. (2025), AI EDAM (Cambridge UP): compared TRIZ vs. brainstorming in human-agent design collaboration. TRIZ produced more detailed creative expression; brainstorming produced more divergent thinking; no significant difference in originality or flexibility. N is small (design-student scale), single-task.
Vargas Hernandez & Schmidt, J. Mechanical Design (ASME, 2013): "Systematic Ideation Effectiveness Study of TRIZ." TRIZ improved Novelty and Variety but slightly reduced Quantity of ideas vs. ad-hoc ideation. Controlled but ideation-quality metrics, not shipped-product outcomes.
Effect on engineering-student creativity (ScienceDirect, 2016): nonequivalent pretest-posttest, N=121; TRIZ increased creative-product scores. Educational outcome, not industrial.
Čok (2026), Proc. Design Society (Cambridge): TRIZ-C + LLM. LLMs did not always improve design quality for complex tasks but reduced cognitive workload and improved contradiction understanding; "prompting experience strongly influenced outcomes".

The matrix-reliability finding (most damaging single data point):

Mann's reverse-engineering of 100 post-2003 patents: Altshuller's original 39×39 matrix predicted the inventive principle actually used in only ~27% of cases (55 of ~206 principles). Mann's own "Matrix 2003" scored 96% — but Mann himself wrote: "Any attempted validation of a tool conducted by the author of that tool is inevitably going to face criticisms of bias… there have thus far been no volunteers to conduct such work.". An earlier Mann investigation recorded ~50% for the original matrix. So the matrix's real predictive hit-rate is somewhere in 27–50%, and the only "high" number is self-validated by the seller. [single-source, author-self-validated]

The critical reviews:

Ilevbare, Probert & Phaal (2013), Technovation — "A review of TRIZ, and its benefits and challenges in practice." Key findings: TRIZ "appears to be a complex approach that requires substantial effort and commitment to understand and lacks an accepted standard for its application"; benefits are dominated by self-reported case studies; practical difficulties "largely neglected by TRIZ literature". (Note: your brief names "Ilevicky/Moody"; the verified source is Ilevbare/Probert/Phaal.)
"Barriers and enablers of TRIZ" (Emerald JEDT, 2022), using the TASKS framework: "TRIZ has limitations on its logic and tools," though it can beneficially affect designers' mental performance.
Irina (2002), TRIZ Journal: practitioner lament on adoption barriers — "fashion fatigue," certification cost, vocabulary overhead.

The three structural critiques (steel-manned):

Post-hoc rationalization. The 40 principles are so broad ("segmentation," "the other way round," "do it in advance") that nearly any solution can be relabelled as an instance of one. This is the "Barnum/Forer" objection: a taxonomy that explains everything predicts little. The 27% matrix hit-rate is the empirical shadow of this.
Patent-sample selection bias. Altshuller mined "strong" (Level 3+) patents, so the principles describe inventive jumps, not the ordinary engineering that is 99% of debugging/ops work. Extrapolating to "how do I keep a watchdog alive" is a domain shift the founders never validated.
No randomized industrial trials. There is no published RCT comparing TRIZ to "ask an expert" or "5 Whys" on MTTR, defect rate, or incident count in a software/SRE setting. The Intel ($212.5M) and Samsung ($100M+) figures are self-reported by the organizations selling/internalizing TRIZ — single-source, low-authority for causal claims.

Honest summary of A3: the evidence base supports TRIZ as a moderate ideation aid (better novelty/variety, no originality edge, slightly fewer ideas). It does not support TRIZ as a superior root-cause or reliability method vs. cheaper alternatives. B3. The four contradictions worked end-to-end

I will work each one in actual TRIZ formulation and then deliver the blunt judgment you asked for.

Contradiction 1 — Watchdog independence vs. internal visibility

Technical contradiction form: Improving parameter = reliability/accuracy of monitoring (Param 27, "Reliability") worsens parameter = independence of the measuring system (≈ Param 28, "Accuracy of measurement" / or a coupling parameter). The matrix would suggest principles like #3 (Local quality), #10 (Preliminary action), #16 (Partial/excessive actions), #32 (Change color/optical properties) — i.e., weak, generic nudges.

Physical contradiction form: The watchdog must be present (to see internal state) and must be absent (to not fail with the watched thing).

Separation principle that resolves it: Separation in space + separation between system and its parts. The watchdog runs on a different machine (space separation) and observes internal state via a side-channel artifact the watched system emits as a normal by-product — a heartbeat written to the Syncthing vault or a file the watched machine drops on schedule. The watched thing does not "know" it is being watched; the artifact is a part of the watched system but the consumer of that artifact is external. This is textbook separation-between-system-and-parts.

IFR: "The watchdog does not exist, yet the moment the watched system stops, an alert is already in the owner's hand." Concretely: the absence of an expected heartbeat file is the alert — no watcher process needed on the watched machine, and the watching side is just "does this file's mtime exceed threshold," a 3-line script on a separate host.

Blunt judgment: TRIZ relabelled what any competent SRE reaches for (out-of-band heartbeat / dead-man's switch). The separation vocabulary is a tidy way to name the move, but the move itself — "put the heartbeat on another box" — is obvious without TRIZ. Value added: naming discipline, not invention. IFR framing ("the watchdog does not exist") is the one piece that might push you toward the dead-man's-switch form rather than an active poller, which is a non-trivial nudge.

Contradiction 2 — Real-human appearance vs. unattended operation

Technical contradiction: Improving adaptability/Universality (Param 35) or manufacturability of the real-looking session worsens Automation / human involvement (Param 36, "Device complexity" or a "human presence" parameter).

Physical contradiction: The session must be a logged-in human (cookies, fingerprint, interaction cadence) and must not be a human (no human present at 3am).

Separation principle: Separation in time + separation on condition. Split the "human-likeness" into a stored artifact (the profile/cookie vault, captured once by a real human act) and a replayable behavioral layer (cadence, jitter, mouse curves) that the automation re-emits later. The human-ness is frozen in time (captured at auth) and replayed on condition (when the bot runs). A second separation: the credential lives as a real human session in space (the profile dir), the driver is a separate process that borrows it.

IFR: "There is no bot and no human; the subscription feature simply arrives in the vault on schedule." That points toward: have the human's own occasional real session produce the artifact (a download, a token refresh), and let automation only transport it — collapsing "bot that impersonates human" into "human who occasionally drops a payload."

Blunt judgment: Mixed. The time-separation insight ("capture the human act once, replay it") is the non-obvious move that breaks the "bot must log in like a human every time" trap, and it is genuinely the architecture that survives bans (persistent profile, periodic human re-auth). But every team that has run browser automation for >6 months converges on persistent profiles + human re-auth windows without TRIZ. TRIZ earns its keep here mostly through the IFR reframing ("maybe the bot shouldn't authenticate at all"), which can dissolve the problem. The matrix itself contributed nothing.

Contradiction 3 — All-machines delivery vs. partial/receive-only offline

Technical contradiction: Improving Completeness of delivery (≈ Param 27 Reliability, or "Volume of moving object") worsens Automation / complexity given heterogeneous availability.

Physical contradiction: The fix must reach all 5 machines and must reach only the ones currently online (because pushing to offline machines blocks/fails).

Separation principle: Separation in space + separation on condition. Don't push; make the fix a pull-shaped artifact in a shared location (the Syncthing vault / a git remote), and let each machine apply it on condition (when it next comes online and notices a new version). The "all 5" requirement is satisfied eventually; the "offline now" requirement is satisfied by making the recipient the active party.

IFR: "The fix is applied the instant it is written, on every machine, by no mechanism." Concretely: a desired-state file in the vault + an idempotent apply routine each machine runs on wake. The fix is the file; application is a property of the machine noticing.

Blunt judgment: TRIZ relabelled the obvious answer (declarative desired-state / pull-based config, the core idea behind Ansible/gitops/cron-apply). No competent engineer needs the matrix to reach "don't push, let them pull." IFR again adds a small nudge toward idempotency ("apply is a no-op if already done"). Minimal TRIZ contribution.

Contradiction 4 — Quiet-when-fine vs. silence ≠ dead

Technical contradiction: Improving convenience/noise (≈ Param 33 "Ease of operation" / or "loss of information") worsens Reliability of the indicator itself (Param 27).

Physical contradiction: The indicator must be silent (when fine) and must be audible (so you know it's alive).

Separation principle: Separation in time (the classic heartbeat / watchdog timer) — silence is the fine-state signal, but a separate periodic "I am alive" pulse on a different channel and cadence breaks the ambiguity. Alternatively separation in space: the health indicator and the liveness indicator are two different artifacts on two different machines.

IFR: "The absence of a message is the message, and the message's absence is impossible to confuse with the channel being dead, by no mechanism." This points to a dead-man's switch with a deadline: the fine-state is "a heartbeat arrived in the last N minutes"; the absence-of-heartbeat is itself the alarm, so there is no separate 'indicator' that can die silently. This dissolves the contradiction rather than resolving it.

Blunt judgment: This is the one case where TRIZ's IFR genuinely produced a non-obvious move: the dead-man's-switch / heartbeat-as-alarm pattern is not the first thing a naive implementer reaches for (they build a green light that can itself go dark). The IFR prompt ("the indicator does not exist but its function is performed") is what forces the collapse. This is TRIZ earning its keep. The separation principle merely names it.

C. The Collision with Radical Simplicity
C1. Where "ideality" and "AK-47" diverge

They sound aligned — both want "less mechanism" — but they optimize different objectives and the divergence is sharp:

Ideality = maximize useful function while driving cost + harm toward zero, with the asymptote "the system does not exist but the function is performed." It rewards elegance and coupling: if one component can do three functions, ideality goes up — even if that component is now a black box a novice cannot reason about.
AK-47 = maximize repairability by a non-engineer with a hammer and a screwdriver. It rewards transparency, decoupling, and dumbness: if three separate dumb components each do one obvious thing, repairability goes up — even if that is "less ideal" by TRIZ's ratio.

Concrete case where TRIZ-ideal is less repairable: Principle #7 (Nested doll) and Principle #6 (Universality) push toward a single component that serves many roles. A watchdog that is also the message bus that is also the config store is more "ideal" (fewer parts, more function per part) but is less AK-47 — when it breaks, the novice cannot isolate which of three roles failed. The TRIZ-ideal solution is more coupled, more clever, and less hammer-repairable. This is the core divergence: ideality optimizes the function-to-parts ratio; AK-47 optimizes the diagnosis-to-parts ratio. They agree only when "fewer parts" also means "fewer coupled roles," which is true for trimming but false for nesting/universality/intermediary.

C2. Documented failure mode: TRIZ producing over-clever solutions

The critique is not just theoretical; it maps onto a well-known software-engineering literature:

Gall's Law (John Gall, Systemantics): "A complex system that works is invariably found to have evolved from a simple system that worked. A complex system designed from scratch never works and cannot be patched up to make it work." This is a direct strike at TRIZ's inventive posture: TRIZ encourages designing the inventive solution, while Gall says working complexity must be grown from working simplicity.
"Worse is better" / New Jersey vs. MIT (Richard Gabriel, 1989): simple, hacked-together software that ships first outcompetes elegant designs because "implementation simplicity" beats "interface simplicity". TRIZ's ideality leans MIT (the right thing, elegant); AK-47 leans New Jersey (dumb, ships, repairable).
Chesterton's Fence: "Reforms should not be made until the reasoning behind the existing state of affairs is understood". TRIZ's trimming impulse can collide with this — aggressively removing "redundant" components that exist precisely to absorb failure modes the trimmer hasn't understood.
Practitioner critiques: Ilevbare et al. (2013) note TRIZ's outputs can be "over-complex" relative to need and that the methodology itself is "a complex approach that requires substantial effort". The Barriers and Enablers review (Emerald 2022) finds "TRIZ has limitations on its logic and tools" — a measured way of saying the cleverness isn't always justified.
"Clever code is unmaintainable" is the software-engineering commonplace: the cleverer the solution, the higher the bus factor and the diagnosis cost. This is the operational shadow of the TRIZ critique.

Failure-mode shape: TRIZ-induced over-cleverness typically looks like (a) a single component serving multiple functions (universality/nesting), (b) a hidden intermediary or phase-transition that "automagically" handles a case, or (c) a feedback loop added to "self-correct" — each of which adds hidden state and coupling that a novice cannot trace at 3am. This is the exact anti-pattern your doctrine exists to prevent.

C4. Is "trimming" the AK-47 inside TRIZ? And when trimming backfires

Largely yes, with one critical caveat. Trimming — eliminating a component while reassigning its useful function to existing components or to the super-system — is the TRIZ move most aligned with "remove mechanism". The ideality trend and trimming together are the closest TRIZ gets to your doctrine.

But the TRIZ literature itself acknowledges trimming can increase fragility in two named ways:

Function reassignment creates coupling. When you trim component A and hand its function to component B, B now does two things. The trimmed system has fewer parts but more coupling per part — exactly the C1 divergence. A 2013 trimming case (Taiwanese foundry slit-valve) explicitly notes trimming re-designed the machine with "fewer components and less cost" but required a non-trivial re-design to preserve function — i.e., trimming is not free; it trades part-count for design-coupling.
Trimming removes redundancy that was load-bearing. This is the Chesterton's-Fence collision: a component that looks redundant may be absorbing a failure mode you haven't characterized. Aggressive trimming without a function model that captures harm-absorption functions (not just useful functions) strips out exactly the "belt-and-suspenders" that makes a system novice-repairable.

When trimming makes a system MORE fragile (synthesis): when (a) the trimmed function is redundancy/failure-absorption rather than a primary useful function, (b) the reassignment concentrates two functions in one component (coupling), or (c) the trimmed component was the only externally-observable signal of a failure (you've trimmed your own instrumentation). Your "green indicator, dead pipeline" class is precisely the case where trimming the indicator would be a disaster. So: trimming is AK-47-aligned only when the trimmed component is genuinely redundant and its function is reassigned to a component that remains single-purpose. Confidence: [emerging] — the fragility modes are named in the trimming literature but not quantified in software contexts.

C5. Adoption cost and the coherence of partial adoption

Learning-curve reality: MATRIZ certification is Level 1 (~16–40 hrs) through Level 5 (TRIZ Master, years). The Ilevbare et al. (2013) review's central finding is that TRIZ "requires substantial effort and commitment to understand and lacks an accepted standard". SIT was created precisely because full TRIZ is too heavy — "SIT can be learned within a couple of hours, TRIZ mastering requires months and years". For a non-engineer owner, full TRIZ is not on the table.

Is partial adoption coherent? Yes — and the evidence, while thin, leans for it rather than against:

SIT and USIT are themselves principled partial adoptions of TRIZ, deliberately stripped to a few templates, and they have independent uptake and case material. So "use a subset" is a recognized, legitimate posture, not a bastardization.
The contradiction + IFR + trimming subset is internally coherent: contradiction-formulation is the entry, IFR sets the direction, trimming is the simplification move. These three form a closed loop that does not require the matrix, Su-Field, ARIZ, or the 76 standards. Dropping the matrix is especially defensible given its 27–50% hit-rate.
The "TRIZ-Lite does nothing" critique is real but mis-aimed at this subset. The critique applies when people wave around "TRIZ principles" as brainstorming prompts without the contradiction-formulation discipline — that is indeed vacuous. The subset proposed here is not "TRIZ-Lite as prompt list"; it is "TRIZ-Lite as questioning discipline": the value is in the act of formulating the contradiction and the IFR, not in retrieving principle #17. Čok (2026) found the contradiction-understanding gain is real even when LLMs don't improve final design quality.

Honest verdict on partial adoption: coherent and defensible, provided the discipline is in the formulation step (contradiction + IFR), not in the matrix-lookup step. Confidence: [emerging].

D2. The minimal ritual (one page)

THE CONTRADICTION CARD — apply when a failure has recurred ≥2×

Name the failure in one sentence. (e.g., "Browser session dies silently; pipeline shows green.")
Find the contradiction. Finish this sentence: "I need X, but X makes Y worse." Then sharpen to the physical form: "The [thing] must be A and must be not-A."
Try the four separations. Can the contradiction be split — in time (do A now, not-A later)? In space (A here, not-A there)? On condition (A when P, not-A when not-P)? Between system and parts (A is a property of the whole, not-A of a part)?
State the IFR. "The [system] does not exist, but its function is performed." What would that look like concretely in your fleet?
Trim before you add. Before adding any mechanism, ask: which existing component could absorb this function? Would removing something dissolve it? (Chesterton check: do I understand why the thing I'm trimming exists?)
Filter against the red-list. If the candidate solution uses nesting, universality, intermediary, feedback, self-service, phase-transition, or composite — stop and justify why the added coupling is worth it, in writing, as a COMPLICATION flag.
Pick the move a non-engineer can repair with a hammer and a screwdriver. If two solutions dissolve the contradiction, choose the dumber one.

Worked example — Contradiction 4 (quiet-when-fine vs. silence ≠ dead), end to end:

Failure: "Indicator is silent, but I can't tell if that means 'fine' or 'indicator itself dead.'"
Contradiction: Technical: improving quiet-when-fine worsens indicator-reliability. Physical: the indicator must be silent (fine) and must be non-silent (alive).
Separations: Time — silent most of the time, noisy on a schedule. Space — the liveness signal on a different machine than the health signal.
IFR: "The indicator does not exist, but its function is performed." → The absence of an expected heartbeat is the alarm; there is no separate indicator that can die.
Trim: Remove the green-indicator component entirely. Replace with: a heartbeat file the watched machine writes every N minutes; the watching machine (different host) alarms on missing heartbeat. This trims the indicator and reassigns its function to the absence of a file.
Red-list check: Does this add nesting/intermediary/feedback? No — it removes a component and adds one dumb file write. Passes.
AK-47 check: A non-engineer can see "file not updated since Tuesday" and understand the alarm. Chosen.

Outcome: the contradiction is dissolved, not compromised. There is no "indicator that can die" anymore; the dead state is the signal. This is the case where TRIZ's IFR genuinely produced a non-obvious move (B3, Contradiction 4).

D3. When TRIZ would be the WRONG choice — and the strongest argument against my recommendation

TRIZ is the wrong choice for you if:

Your failures are predominantly linear cause-effect (a config typo, a wrong path, an expired token) rather than contradictions. For those, 5-Whys / KT is/is-not / hypothesis-driven debugging is faster, cheaper, and better-evidenced. Forcing a contradiction formulation onto a non-contradiction is the "TRIZ-Lite does nothing" failure mode.
Your team cannot spare even the ~8 hours for the ritual, or won't actually write the card. Half-applied TRIZ-as-jargon is worse than no TRIZ because it lends false structure.
You start reaching for the matrix or Su-Field — at that point you've taken on the vocabulary tax without the evidence payoff (27–50% matrix hit-rate, no software-ops validation).

The strongest argument against my recommendation (steel-manned opposition): "You are recommending a subset of TRIZ, but the subset's value is unproven as a subset. The controlled studies that show any TRIZ benefit (Ge 2025, Vargas Hernandez 2013) used TRIZ as taught, with the matrix and the full principle set. There is no study showing that contradiction + IFR + trimming alone outperforms plain hypothesis-driven debugging on MTTR or reopen rate. You may be prescribing a sugar pill with Soviet branding. The honest move is Option 1 (no TRIZ) plus a written dead-man's-switch rule, which captures 90% of the dissolution value in D2's worked example without importing any TRIZ vocabulary or risk of cultish drift."

This is a serious objection. The defense is that (a) the mechanism by which the subset helps — forcing explicit contradiction articulation before fix-attempts — is independently supported by the debugging literature (a stated hypothesis is more falsifiable than an unstated one), and (b) the IFR prompt specifically forces the "dissolve, don't compromise" move that 5-Whys does not force. But the objection is correct that the 30-day measurement in D1 is the real test; if Option 2 doesn't beat Option 1 on reopen rate, the honest conclusion is that the TRIZ subset added nothing and Option 1 was the answer all along.
